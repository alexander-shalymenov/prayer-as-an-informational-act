Prayer as an Informational Act

F(Ω) = K(Ω) − p(Ω)
Shalymenov's Criterion · Why some prayers succeed and others fail

How prayer works according to Informational Actualism

Prayer is not magic. It is an informational act that modifies the subject's own configuration within the space of possibilities (Ω). The treatise (Chapter IV) explains: “Prayer lowers the cost of a determinate state if it renders the subject more aligned with that state. It clarifies intention, reduces inner contradiction, disciplines attention, and links desire with action.”

Ω (space of possibilities) – all admissible future states. Physics describes transitions but not why one becomes fact. Prayer operates within Ω, not against it.

K(Ω) (algorithmic complexity) – the cost of description. Extra wishes, contradictions, or vague clauses increase K. A clean, direct prayer keeps K low.

p(Ω) (probabilistic noise / surprise) – the cost of improbability. Words like “maybe”, “if possible”, contradictory desires raise p.

Shalymenov's Criterion: The factual state (what actually happens) is the one that minimizes total cost:
F = K(Ω) − p(Ω)
→ Reality selects the state with the lowest F. A prayer modifies K and p for the desired outcome. Only an exact, noise‑free prayer makes the desired F smaller than the F of the alternative (“nothing changes”).

Therefore, prayers fail when they contain extra wishes, contradictions, or vague language — because those increase K and p, making the desired outcome more expensive than doing nothing.

Key insight from Chapter IV
• A prayer works as a structured prompt (API call to reality).
• It does not violate physics but modifies the subject’s configuration, lowering the cost of a possible future.
• Extra wishes, contradictions, or vague language increase algorithmic cost K and noise p, making the desired outcome more expensive than alternatives.
• Only exact, disciplined prayers succeed — they minimize K and p just enough.
